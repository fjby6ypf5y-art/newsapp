Your feeds

Tap a feed to edit its name, address or category. A green dot means it answered on the last refresh; red shows what went wrong. Feeds are fetched from your phone, so this is the only place the result is actually true — a feed that works on one network can fail on another.

Settings

Behaviour

Reopening the app after this long goes back to the first category instead of wherever you were. Pull down on the list to refresh just the category you're reading; the ↻ button refreshes every category.

Privacy

Fetch relay

News sites block direct browser requests (CORS). Each feed is tried directly first, then through the relays — one URL-prefix per line, fastest-first from what they have actually done on this phone, several at a time rather than one after another. A relay that has just failed twice sits out for ten minutes instead of costing every remaining feed a timeout, and a feed on a relay is re-tested directly every few hours in case the publisher adds CORS support.

A relay sees your IP address and which feeds you fetch, so the fewer feeds that need one, the less anybody learns about your reading. Only full https:// prefixes are kept, five at most; leave the box empty to use no relay at all.

Backup

Keep this link somewhere. A Home Screen app has its own storage on iOS, separate from Safari, and deleting the icon deletes your feeds with it. Opening the setup link offers to restore your feeds and settings, so add that link to the Home Screen after a reinstall and you are back where you were. Mail it to yourself; it is just a long URL.

OPML is the standard feed-list format: export it to move your feeds into any other reader, or paste one in to bring feeds across. Importing adds to your list rather than replacing it, and skips feeds you already have. Folder names that match a category are kept; anything else lands in Other.

Diagnostics

The health dots on the Feeds page say whether a feed answered the last time it was asked. The log keeps the last 60 failures and recoveries with the full detail — every relay tried, what each one said, how long it took — which is the difference between "this feed is broken" and "this feed fails every third refresh through one particular relay".
